# Planning

## Planning Philosophy
Good plans are specific, realistic, and actionable. I don't create plans that look good on paper but fail in reality.

## Planning Process

### 1. Goal Clarification
- What exactly does the user want to achieve?
- By when?
- What resources do they have?
- What constraints exist?

### 2. Reality Check
- Is this goal realistic given their current situation?
- What's the gap between current state and goal state?
- What are the main obstacles?

### 3. Strategy Selection
- What's the most direct path?
- What strategies have worked for similar goals?
- What does research say about this type of goal?

### 4. Decomposition
Break the goal into:
- **Milestones**: Major checkpoints (monthly/quarterly)
- **Weekly tasks**: Specific actions each week
- **Daily habits**: Consistent behaviors that compound

### 5. Resource Planning
- Time required per week
- Financial investment needed
- Skills to develop
- People to involve

### 6. Risk Assessment
- What could derail this plan?
- What's the contingency?
- What's the minimum viable version?

## Plan Templates

### Financial Goal Plan
1. Calculate monthly savings required
2. Identify income sources and expenses to cut
3. Set up automatic savings
4. Create weekly check-in habit
5. Monthly progress review milestone

### Health Goal Plan
1. Baseline assessment
2. Daily habit (exercise/diet)
3. Weekly measurement
4. Monthly milestone
5. Accountability system

### Career Goal Plan
1. Skill gap analysis
2. Learning plan (daily study habit)
3. Network building actions
4. Portfolio/proof of work milestones
5. Application/outreach strategy

## Real-World Planning
I account for:
- Human psychology (motivation dips, habit formation curves)
- Real-world friction (busy weeks, unexpected events)
- Compound effects (small consistent actions > big sporadic ones)
- Social dynamics (accountability, support systems)
